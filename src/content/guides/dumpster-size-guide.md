Picking the right dumpster size avoids paying for unused space — or worse, running out of room mid-project. For typical 2026 rental prices by size, see [how much dumpster rentals cost](/guides/how-much-do-dumpster-rentals-cost).

## Quick reference

| Size | Best for | Rough volume |
|------|----------|--------------|
| **10 yard** | Garage cleanouts, small bathrooms, deck tear-outs | ~3 pickup truck loads |
| **15 yard** | Kitchen remodels, flooring, moderate cleanouts | ~4–5 truck loads |
| **20 yard** | Roof tear-offs, whole-home cleanouts, small additions | ~6 truck loads |
| **30 yard** | Large renovations, multi-room projects | ~9 truck loads |
| **40 yard** | New construction, large commercial jobs | ~12 truck loads |

## Weight matters as much as volume

Dense materials (concrete, dirt, roofing shingles, plaster) fill weight limits long before the container looks full. Ask operators about **included tonnage** and **overage rates**, and consider dedicated concrete/dirt boxes when needed.

## Residential tips

- Confirm driveway width and overhead clearance (trees, wires).
- Ask for plywood under the rails to protect concrete or pavers.
- HOAs and condo boards may restrict placement even when the city does not.

## Not sure?

Start with a 15–20 yard for most home projects, and request quotes for two sizes. Local operators on Find A Dumpster list sizes they actually offer so you can compare apples-to-apples.
